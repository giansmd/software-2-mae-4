# Abstract

Digital twins of natural ecosystems are an emerging paradigm for environmental monitoring and management, yet their application to coastal wetlands of the southeastern Pacific remains scarcely documented. This study presents a **digital twin** of the Los Manglares de Tumbes National Sanctuary (SNLMT), in northern Peru, applying the **CRISP-DM** methodology to model and explain the variation in retained carbon under hydrological restoration and sea-level rise scenarios through 2050.

The dataset is **hybrid** in nature: the covariates and calibration come from published real data on the SNLMT —extent and cover by class, Normalized Difference Vegetation Index (NDVI) ranges, Importance Value Index (IVI) by species and blue carbon content— while the target variable is generated by a deterministic, parameterisable, replicable engine that can be replaced by field measurements. The explicit separation between both portions responds to the transparency requirement that the ecological digital twin literature consistently formulates.

Five supervised learning algorithms were evaluated through ten-fold cross-validation, statistical significance testing (paired t and Wilcoxon), bootstrap resampling confidence intervals, and interpretability via permutation importance. The **Extra Trees** ensemble achieved the best performance (R² = 1.000; MAE = 0.014 percentage points), significantly outperforming the linear baseline (t = 25.91; p < 0.001). Hydrological restoration produced increases above the +25 % threshold in 34.85 % of scenarios in the high-intervention band, with a mean gain of 11.24 %, while the intermediate bands produced the transition from net loss to net gain.

The results are materialised in an interactive dashboard and exportable reports that document each analytical phase, with version control traceability over 39 code commits.

**Keywords:** digital twin; coastal wetland; blue carbon; CRISP-DM; hydrological restoration
