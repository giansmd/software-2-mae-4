# Conclusion

The present work developed a digital twin of the Los Manglares de Tumbes National Sanctuary and applied the CRISP-DM methodology to model, explain and communicate the variation of blue carbon under hydrological restoration and sea-level rise scenarios through 2050. The conclusions are organised around the four success criteria defined in the business understanding phase.

**Predictive performance.** The criterion of an out-of-sample R² greater than or equal to 0.85 was comfortably met. The three evaluated tree ensembles exceeded the threshold, and the Extra Trees model reached R² = 1.000 with a mean absolute error of 0.014 percentage points and a 95 % confidence interval of [0.012; 0.016]. The null standard deviation across the ten cross-validation folds confirms the stability of performance. The criterion of an MAE equal to or below 3 percentage points was also satisfied.

**Statistical superiority.** The criterion of significant difference from the reference linear model was met in all comparisons. The paired t test yielded p < 0.001 against the linear models (t = 25.91) and against the remaining ensembles (t between 5.23 and 7.49). The non-parametric Wilcoxon test converges in the same direction, and the verification of non-normality of the residuals (Shapiro-Wilk, p < 0.001) justifies its inclusion as a complementary contrast.

**Explainability.** Permutation importance identified the sea-level rise scenario (mean importance = 1.071) and the hydrological restoration level (0.839) as the only variables with relevant contribution, with reduced standard deviations. This result coincides exactly with the algebraic structure of the engine, which constitutes an indirect validation of the attribution procedure and a practical demonstration that interpretability can audit black-box models.

**Hypothesis contrast.** The hypothesis that hydrological restoration can increase carbon retention by at least 25 % by 2050 **is not verified in general**. It is met conditionally: in the highest restoration band (75–100 %), 34.85 % of scenarios exceed the threshold, with a mean gain of 11.24 %. In contrast, the medium restoration bands produce the transition from net loss to net gain without reaching the aspirational threshold.

These findings must be interpreted in the light of the study's main limitation: the target variable is simulated. The conclusions describe the fidelity of the emulator relative to the deterministic engine and the internal coherence of the physical model, not the predictive capacity over field observations. The substitution of the calibrated parameters by measurements from the SNLMT —currently under way through the region's blue carbon certification process [9]— constitutes the necessary step to confer empirical validity on the projections.

Beyond the numerical results, the work contributes three reusable products: a hybrid dataset anchored in published real data with explicit traceability of its components; an analytical module that implements the six phases of CRISP-DM in a reproducible and verifiable manner; and an interactive dashboard that exposes interpretability as a first-class capability, allowing non-specialised managers to interrogate the model and understand the contribution of each variable to the prediction.

The practical relevance of the approach lies in its capacity to sustain management decisions with quantified and communicable evidence. An instrument that makes it possible to estimate the restoration level required to transition from net loss to net carbon gain, and that makes explicit the dependence of that response on the climate scenario, has direct value for conservation planning, investment prioritisation and the technical substantiation of blue carbon certification processes.

Finally, the version control discipline sustained throughout development —39 commits with descriptive messages linking each change to the finding that motivated it— constitutes a methodological contribution in itself: it guarantees that any reported result is traceable to the exact state of the code that produced it, an indispensable condition for the reproducibility of computational research.
