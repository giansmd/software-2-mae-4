# Material and Methods

## Study area and data sources

The study was conducted on the Los Manglares de Tumbes National Sanctuary (SNLMT), located in the district of Zarumilla, department of Tumbes, Peru. The Sanctuary encompasses 2 972 ha, of which 1 779.8 ha correspond to mangrove cover (68.06 %), 243 ha to dry forest, and the remainder to water bodies and saline areas.

## Design of the hybrid dataset

The dataset is hybrid in nature and explicit in its traceability. The **covariates are real**: the ranges of hydrological restoration, wetland area, carbon density and NDVI are anchored in the observed and published values for the SNLMT. The **target variable is simulated**: the percentage variation of retained carbon by 2050 is generated by the digital twin's deterministic engine, whose parameters are calibratable and replaceable by field measurements.

The explicit separation between the real and simulated components responds to a methodological criterion: it allows auditing which part of the result rests on empirical evidence and which part on the physical model's projection. The engine implements a saturating response curve to restoration —consistent with the diminishing returns observed in restored wetlands [10]— and a progressive loss of area and density associated with three sea-level rise scenarios aligned with IPCC ranges [15].

Two thousand records were generated with a fixed seed to guarantee reproducibility. Half of the observations were concentrated at the extremes of the restoration range —below 12 % and above 88 %— given that tree ensembles exhibit bias at the edges of variable domains [24].

## The CRISP-DM protocol

The system implements the standard’s six phases as verifiable modules.

### Phase 1. Business understanding

Business and analytical objectives were defined with *a priori* success criteria: out-of-sample R² ≥ 0.85; MAE ≤ 3 percentage points; significant difference from the linear baseline; and cross-partition R² standard deviation ≤ 0.05.

### Phase 2. Data understanding

Exploratory analysis comprised descriptive statistics, quality diagnostics —missing values, duplicates and cardinality— and Pearson correlation analysis. **Table 3** summarises the behaviour of each variable in the dataset.

### Phase 3. Data preparation

The feature matrix was built with ordinal encoding of the sea-level rise scenario and the target variable was separated. Standardised scaling was applied **within** each modelling pipeline, so that normalisation parameters are estimated exclusively on each training partition and do not contaminate the evaluation — a design that prevents information leakage, a frequent error when scaling is executed on the complete set before partitioning.

### Phase 4. Modelling

Five algorithms representative of three families were compared: a linear model as reference [23], a regularised variant and three tree ensembles [1], [16], [24]. All were implemented as scikit-learn pipelines [3], guaranteeing identical preprocessing and strict comparability.

### Phase 5. Evaluation

The evaluation combined four complementary instruments. First, **cross-validation** with ten folds, employing the mean R² across folds as the principal selection criterion. Second, **statistical significance tests** —paired Student's t and Wilcoxon signed-rank on the per-fold scores— to contrast the best model against the remaining ones [5], [17]. Third, **confidence intervals** of the mean absolute error via bootstrap resampling with 500 replicates [22]. Fourth, **assumption checking** via the Shapiro-Wilk test on the residuals [39], which guides the choice between parametric and non-parametric tests.

**Interpretability** was addressed through permutation importance, a model-agnostic technique quantifying the drop in R² when each variable is randomly shuffled, with ten repetitions to estimate variability [1], [40]. This choice allows auditing the model without depending on assumptions about its internal structure.

### Phase 6. Deployment

Deployment materialised in two products. The first is an **interactive dashboard** developed in Streamlit that exposes the six phases, allows recalculating the pipeline with different hyperparameters, and presents each figure and table with its interpretation. The second is a set of **exportable reports** in CSV and PNG format, plus a Word document generated deterministically from independent Markdown sections.

## Version control and traceability

Development was managed on Git with a linear history on the main branch, accumulating 39 commits between 31 August and 29 September 2026. The message convention followed the conventional commits format, with prefixes identifying the type of change and references to the quality review findings addressed — a practice that made it possible to reconstruct the origin of each design decision and sustain continuous review. Issue management was planned on a tracking platform with a task board, user stories and acceptance criteria; its full implementation fell outside the deliverable's time scope, but the commit-message structure preserves the link between each change and its motivating finding or requirement.

## Reproducibility

The code is organised into modules for hybrid dataset generation, CRISP-DM, the interactive application and document assembly. Article figures derive automatically from the engine. A fixed random seed (42) and pinned dependencies ensure reproducibility.
